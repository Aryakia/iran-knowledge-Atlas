Knowledge-system resilience · public research release v0.7

How do knowledge systems survive history?

Iran Knowledge Atlas is an exploratory System Dynamics research platform examining how knowledge can accumulate, move, face disruption, and recover across Iranian and Persianate history. It combines documentary evidence, transparent reconstruction and selected model experiments without presenting simulated indices as historical measurements.

Interpretation boundary: this project does not estimate historical national IQ. Knowledge Capital and Realized Knowledge Capability are normalized model constructs, not observed psychometric measures or population rankings.

Three research layers

Evidence is not reconstruction. Reconstruction is not simulation.

The public interface deliberately separates different types of claims so a historical source, a modeled reconstruction and a forward experiment are not visually or conceptually conflated.

01 · Evidence

What is historically documented?

Periods, events, scholars, places and source links are presented with citation-status metadata and explicit evidence limits.

02 · Reconstruction

What does the historical model represent?

Normalized Knowledge Capital and Realized Knowledge Capability outputs are conditional on proxy coding and uncalibrated assumptions.

03 · Simulation

What happens under specified experiments?

Named System Dynamics scenarios are precomputed privately and published as trajectories with structural-sensitivity ranges.

Research protection

Public scholarship, private implementation.

The public site is intentionally a distribution layer. It is not a mirror or fork of the canonical research repository.

Public

  • historical descriptions and public citations
  • maps and scholar/event records
  • precomputed reconstructed outputs
  • named scenario trajectories and structural ranges
  • generic presentation code

Private canonical research repository

  • Python and JavaScript model engines
  • equations and coefficients
  • raw driver profiles and event-effect magnitudes
  • sensitivity implementation and tests
  • internal research notes and development history
